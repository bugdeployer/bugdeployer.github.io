Multiplexr: collections, spaces and local playback.

How Multiplexr organizes local media, handles playback and metadata, and what’s available in Preview 6.

Multiplexr is a free, open-source media library for Android. It organizes files you already have on your device into collections, with built-in playback and separate browsing spaces for different kinds of media.

I built it for my own local library. I wanted collections, artwork and playback history in one app, with a choice between a straightforward file-oriented view and a more visual browsing experience.

Collections are the starting point

A collection points to one or more root folders. Multiplexr scans those folders and builds an index; the original media stays where it is.

The current collection choices are Video → Movies & TV, Video → Videos, and Audio → Audiobooks. Movies & TV handles films and series, while Videos suits clips and other videos that do not need that structure. Audiobooks has its own metadata and listening controls.

Collections have search, sorting, filters and tags. You can change their browsing layout, choose the internal or an external player, and set whether tapping a playable title opens its details or starts playback. A series opens its contents so you can choose an episode or audiobook.

Spaces are another way to browse

Cinema, Videos and Audiobooks each have a dedicated space. Cinema presents films and TV series with artwork and browsing rows. Videos uses a video feed. Audiobooks provides listening progress, search and series browsing.

A space can be local to one collection or global across eligible collections. Excluding a collection from a global space does not remove its local space. Display settings include showcase artwork, and you can choose and order up to three showcase titles.

These spaces use the same library underneath. Opening a title from Cinema does not create another copy of it.

Playback and progress

The video player supports resume, subtitles, audio tracks, playback speed and queues. TV series have seasons and episodes, with continuation based on viewing progress. External players are available when you prefer another app.

Audiobooks use series → audiobook → chapters. A standalone audiobook also sits inside a one-title series. A book can be one long M4B or several audio files; chapter navigation belongs in the player rather than appearing as separate books in the library.

Audiobook controls include speed, bookmarks, a sleep timer and background playback. Continue series selects an audiobook to resume, and next-audiobook autoplay can continue within that series.

Metadata is optional

You can keep local information and edit it yourself, or look for metadata online. Movie and TV lookup uses TMDB. Audiobook lookup supports Audible and AudiobookDB, including recording information such as narrator and edition. Provider keys, where required, are entered in Settings → Metadata.

Preview 6 gives movies, TV and audiobooks a shared review-and-apply workflow. Search results are paginated. Before applying a match, you can compare current and proposed values and choose which fields to replace. Manually edited fields and chosen artwork are left unselected by default.

Collection metadata jobs can be cancelled and resumed. They keep completed work, retry temporary network failures within a limit, and stop for credential or rate-limit problems. Ambiguous video matches need review; audiobook matches need review because editions and narrators can differ.

Artwork has separate controls. Audiobook covers can come from online search or a device image. A series can use a cover from one of its audiobooks. Choosing a series cover does not replace its members’ covers, and TV series posters are kept separate from episode posters.

Appearance, locks and backups

Retro uses pixel-style type and icons; Modern provides the other bundled style. Background and accent choices are preserved when switching between them.

App and collection locks use device authentication. Collection locks control access inside Multiplexr; they do not encrypt the source files. Backups save library information and playback history, not the media itself. Provider credentials are excluded from library backups.

Current release and development

At the time of this post, 0.1.0 is the stable release and 0.1.1 Preview 6 contains the newer audiobook and shared-workflow changes described here. Multiplexr requires Android 7.0 or newer. It does not include media or download films and shows.

Recent development has focused on the foundation: shared media contracts, storage coordination, inherited settings, navigation and metadata services. That work lets overlapping features use the same behavior across collection types.

Next is more work on hierarchy and scanning, collection and space consistency, and broader theme styling. Music, photos, ebooks and comics are not ready to use. I’ll write about them as they become available.

Download Preview 6, browse the source code, or report an issue. For a bug report, include the app version, Android version and steps to reproduce it.
